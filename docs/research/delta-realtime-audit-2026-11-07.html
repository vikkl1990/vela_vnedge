Reliability corrections completed locally

  1. Repair each interrupted candle before announcing its close. Recovery has a connection generation and per-series barrier. Completed REST repairs are labelled historical; they cannot fill new pending entries from old OHLC. Failed/empty repairs stay visible and retry. Recovery runs with bounded concurrency instead of serially waiting through the entire universe. server/src/data/candleStore.ts
  2. Preserve newer WS data and coalesce duplicate repairs. A REST response cannot replace a candle updated by WS while that request was in flight, and concurrent resync calls share a request.
  3. Bound failed connections. A stalled handshake times out at 15 seconds; missing heartbeats trigger reconnection at the documented 35-second threshold even if unrelated messages still arrive. server/src/delta/ws.ts
  4. Keep uncertain trades out of execution. Missing trade time, future timestamps, invalid prices and nonpositive quantities cannot trigger fills or tape-audit evidence. Order-flow diagnostics still see rejected observations so their coverage is not falsely certified. server/src/execution/wiring.ts
  5. Keep product snapshots complete. Malformed pages, repeated cursors, empty continuation pages and exhausted pagination fail explicitly; the last complete catalogue is preserved. Concurrent refreshes share one request. Mutating REST requests default to no retry. server/src/delta/rest.ts
  6. Refresh time-quality measurements and funding state correctly. A continuously busy feed can no longer retain an old near-zero clock-drift sample forever. Late or nonfinite funding observations cannot replace a newer valid rate. server/src/data/marks.ts

The practical solution is route selection. For a structurally valid signal, compare taker entry against a post-only maker entry using estimated fill probability, time to fill, adverse selection and net target value. Cancel an unfilled maker order when the setup expires; cross only when the remaining opportunity still covers measured costs. Keep scanner-native stop/target structure and the dollar-risk cap. Assess each pair × scanner × timeframe cohort on identical signals, including unfilled and cancelled orders.

The official Scalper Offer needs opt-in, waives qualifying closing fees within 30 minutes for BTC/ETH and 15 minutes for other eligible futures, and excludes the named metal contracts. The current BTC Diwali benefit returns the opening fee as a voucher, not immediate cash P&L. Keep fee vouchers separate from trading performance. Official offer terms; fee and GST information.

The next implementation, in order

Validated Delta events→Per-symbol state→Entry / exit engine→Durable order intents→Confirmed fills
  1. Apply the tested reliability patch. Verify reconnect recovery against held positions and pending orders. Keep an explicit recovering count in health (the tracked-series response now includes it).
  2. Make execution evidence complete. Record trades plus timestamped L1/L2 for all held or pending pairs and the selected top-20 crypto research universe. Use daily compressed segments with per-symbol gap manifests and snapshots for replay; keep SQLite for summaries and ledgers. Reconcile subscriptions to the active universe so retired symbols do not accumulate forever. Retain enough days to compare full trades, not just the latest hour.
  3. Build one causal exit engine. Trade time establishes a last-price trigger; mark price is used for liquidation, and must be fresh. Execute stop/trailing market exits on the current bid/ask and quantity-aware depth. Track requested, filled and remaining quantity. Maker limits need queue/partial-fill evidence; a price touch alone is insufficient. Keep candle fallback explicitly labelled and outside tick-quality comparisons. Do not simply flip fillSource: tape stops currently still use print-plus-slippage.
  4. Run paired replay and a prospective comparison. Same signal timestamp, bound policy, $150 margin sizing, 3% risk cap and fee schedule for both models. Compare candle versus causal execution, then test passive entry separately. Preserve unfilled trades, missed coverage, funding and adverse stop slippage. Report net expectancy, stop rate, captured profit, fill rate, drawdown, turnover and decision-to-fill latency by scanner/pair/TF. Choose on held-out periods and then paper-forward evidence; do not optimize against the same history used to select the rule.
  5. Add private exchange events before live trading. Use current key-auth for orders/fills/positions/wallet events, backed by REST reconciliation and the existing durable intent ledger. Add exchange-native reduce-only stops and an order-acknowledgement/protection state machine. Exercise disconnect during partial fill, process crash after order submission, rejected stops and restart recovery on testnet. No claim of production readiness until these paths pass.
  6. Expose real health and share API capacity. Show quote/trade/mark age separately, missing subscriptions, recovery count, REST 429/reset state, queue wait and fallback exits. A shared weighted REST budget should prioritize protection/reconciliation, then entries, then research. The current client honors reset headers per request but there is no cross-process budget across the bot and screening jobs.

These changes improve the fidelity and resilience of execution. Whether they increase profitable trade counts requires the paired replay and forward comparison. The feed measurements alone do not establish a trading edge.
