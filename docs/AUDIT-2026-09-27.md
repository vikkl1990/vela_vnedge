# Audit — scanners and exits — 27 September 2026

What is actually true about how this bot finds trades and gets out of them, checked against the
running machine and the measurements, with the mistakes named. `SYSTEM.md` remains the contract;
this is the examination of it. Where this document and `SYSTEM.md` disagree, this one is newer and
`SYSTEM.md` is to be corrected.

## The ten findings

1. **The best scanner's backtest was reading fifty bars into the future.** `structure-anchored-vwap`
   is traded from a rule on its swing-structure labels, not from its own signals, and the rule shifted
   the label by 5 bars where the script confirms a pivot only after 55. Corrected, its edge falls from
   **+0.70R to +0.10R per trade** (decision 44). Every claim built on it — "81% of the fleet's edge",
   the fade result in decision 33, the stop width in decision 43 — is re-stated below.
2. **Its own Buy/Sell signals have never fired in our runtime.** Zero alerts and zero plotshapes on
   every market and window tested. What trades under that name is our rule, on its labels.
3. **No scanner in the fleet supplies a stop or a target.** Across eight fleet scanners and 1,867
   entries on two markets: 0 with a stop, 0 with a target. The ATR fallback and the 2/4/6R ladder are
   not a fallback; they are the whole exit policy, for every trade.
4. **The exit is near its ceiling.** If every winner kept 100% of its peak instead of 58%, expectancy
   at the measured 36% win rate would still be **−0.081R**. Eight exit ideas were tested and lost; three
   were adopted and are worth about +0.025R together. The money is in what is traded, not how it exits.
5. **Seventeen of 24 scanner × timeframe combinations lose in both halves of history**, and the fleet
   runs every one of them on 15m, where the measured edge is weakest.
6. **The account, not the strategy, is choosing trades.** 65% of signals on the 33-pair fleet were
   refused for margin or the position cap; the daily 15% loss limit fired 17 times this week; equity
   is $840 against a $1,003 peak and every position is being sized at 50%.
7. **The pipeline is fixed.** Stale rejections were 48% of signals; in the last 48 hours they are 0%.
   The weekend block refused 22% of opportunities for no measured reason and is gone. 427 scripts that
   can never run here are quarantined.
8. **The runtime silently loses scripts.** Some run, report success, and emit nothing — including the
   best scanner's native signals, the one new WillyAlgoTrader script, and BigBeluga's levels. The
   nightly screen took **207 minutes** last night.
9. **The VM ran day-old code for a day.** Every deploy after decision 36 aborted on an untracked file
   and my verification read the wrong line. Fixed, and the check is now `rev-list --count`, not the
   pull's output.
10. **There is no forward evidence for the best scanner.** Zero trades in the live book, zero in the
    shadow book. Everything known about it is backtest.

### 1.3 The lookahead (decision 44)

`structure-anchored-vwap` draws `HH / HL / LH / LL` labels at swing pivots. The rule in
`scanners/rules.ts` reads `HL` as long and `LH` as short, anchored to the pivot bar plus `delayBars`.
The worker records a label's **anchor** time, not the bar it was created on, and the script's pivots
use **55 bars each side** (`pivRInput = 55`). With `delayBars: 5`, the backtest entered fifty bars
before the pivot could have been known.

Corrected on eight markets, 12,000 bars of 15m, fallback stop 4×ATR:

| delay | trades | avg R | second half |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 5 (as measured until today) | 433 | **+0.697R** | +0.647R |
| **55 (the script's own confirmation)** | 435 | **+0.099R** | +0.211R |

The halves test did not catch this and could not: both halves leaked equally. It is the one failure
mode that discipline does not defend against, and it needs its own check (§5).

Live trading was **not** affected — the live path fires on the bar a label first appears — and the
`auto-s-r-channels` rule anchors at the creation bar, so it is correct at `delayBars: 0`. A second,
smaller leak was closed at the same time: a pivot whose confirming bars lay beyond the window used to
be traded at the window's last bar; it is now not traded.

### 1.4 What the corrected numbers still support

against −10.9R), so the override stands as **moderate** evidence rather than the decisive kind

### 1.6 The incubator

Works as designed since decision 29: 100 shadow slots, cohort gate pooled per scanner × timeframe,
15m cohorts reach a judgeable sample in **3 days**, 1h in 17, 4h in 53. 314 shadow trades so far.
Two inconsistencies remain: the shadow book does **not** pass the risk gate and does **not** write to

### 1.7 Runtime

Scripts that hang or throw are quarantined. Scripts that **run and emit nothing** are not detected:

arrays, the same family as the 84 quarantined runtime gaps. The nightly screen is at 207 minutes and
growing with the library.

### 2.3 The ceiling

At the measured 36% win rate and −0.86R average loss, break-even needs a **+1.68R** average win. The
fleet produces +0.85R. Winners keep a median 58% of their peak; if they kept **all** of it the average
win would be +1.47R and expectancy still **−0.081R**. No exit rule closes that gap. Fees are 0.066R a
trade — 8% of it.

### 2.4 Stops specifically

Half of all trades hit the stop and 71% of those were in profit first, median peak +0.25R. Every
rule that acts in that band loses. Wider stops are decisively worse for the fleet (monotone, and
past 2.5×ATR they lose money outright); tighter is a tie in dollars from a quarter fewer trades.
Script-published stops are worse than the ATR fallback on 320 live trades (−0.349R against −0.157R)
and there are almost none of them anyway.

## 3. What the account is doing to the results

- Equity **$840** against a 7-day peak of $1,003; the drawdown scaler has every position at **50%**.
- The daily loss limit fired **17 times** this week.
- On the 33-pair fleet, **65%** of signals were refused for capacity.
- One AKEUSD contract is 95% of the risk budget; the account can take one or none.

Backtests assume none of this. Live results are running at half size, through a kill switch, on a
book that refuses two thirds of what the strategy wants to do. The paper account has to decide what
it is for: a model of a real $1,000 account, in which case ~1.4 trades a day is the honest answer, or
an instrument for judging scanners, in which case capacity must stop filtering.

## 4. Architecture: what to change, in order

Each item names the acceptance test that would show it worked.

**1. Re-establish what the fleet's edge is, without the leak.** Re-run the fleet survivors with the
corrected rule and read the halves table again; re-state decisions 33 and 43 for
`structure-anchored-vwap` from the corrected numbers. *Accepted when* the per-timeframe halves table
in this document is filled and `SYSTEM.md`'s "established" section matches it.

**2. Put the surviving fleet through the shadow book, on the timeframes where it survives.** The
cohort gate returns a 15m verdict in 3 days. Nothing else in this repo turns "chosen on a backtest"
into "proved afterwards". *Accepted when* every live pair has a cohort verdict behind it.

**3. Decide the account model, then size the paper purse to it.** Either raise `initialEquity` and
the position cap until capacity stops filtering, or prune the fleet to what $1,000 can carry.
*Accepted when* capacity rejections fall below 10% of signals.

**4. Detect silent scripts.** A script that runs successfully on N markets and emits nothing
(no alert, shape, label or plot) is a runtime gap, not a selective strategy; mark it as such so the
screen stops paying for it and the count of unlocked scripts becomes a known number. *Accepted when*
`npm run health` lists "silent" alongside "quarantined".

**5. Make the shadow book the same bot.** Route shadow entries through the risk gate and write them
to the signals table. *Accepted when* a shadow trade and a live trade on the same signal differ only
in book number.

**6. Guard label rules against the leak by construction.** The worker should record the bar a label
was **created** on; rules then need no `delayBars` at all. Until then, every label rule's delay must
equal the script's pivot right-bars, and the test suite should assert it for each. *Accepted when* the
worker exposes `createdAt` and the rules test compares rule delays to script inputs.

**7. Give the nightly screen a budget.** 207 minutes is the wrong direction. Skip silent scripts,
cap per-script runtime below the pool timeout, and record per-script cost so the slowest are visible.
*Accepted when* a full slice completes inside 90 minutes.

**8. Freeze the exit policy.** It is near its ceiling and every remaining idea has been measured.
Further exit work should come only from the path recorder — the manual-close study once there are
a few weeks of recorded paths.

**9. Fix alerting and deploy verification.** Telegram token in the systemd environment; every deploy
verified by `git rev-list --count HEAD..origin/main` returning 0 and a marker string in the served
bundle. The second is done as of today.

## 5. Measurement discipline, amended

The rules that held up this month, plus the one that today showed was missing:

1. Fit on one half, judge on the other, and require the **same sign** in both.
2. Report **total R and dollars**, not R per trade alone — filters that trade less flatter themselves.
3. Compare **one width for all** against any per-market fit before believing the fit.
4. **Check the anchor.** For any rule built on drawn objects, confirm the object's time is when it
   became knowable, not where it points. The halves test is blind to this; only reading the script's
   confirmation parameters catches it.
5. Verify a deploy by the commit count, not by the pull's output.

## Appendix — `structure-anchored-vwap`, corrected, 28 markets × 3 timeframes

Same survey that produced the fleet, re-run with the rule anchored at the script's own confirmation
(`delayBars: 55`), 4.0×ATR stop, halves pooled by trades:

| tf | markets | trades | total R | avg R | markets positive | 1st half avg R | 2nd half avg R | positive in both halves |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **15m** (where the fleet runs it) | 28 | 854 | **+6.3R** | +0.007 | 15 | +0.017 | **−0.002** | 5 |
| 1h | 28 | 773 | +52.6R | +0.068 | 18 | +0.147 | **−0.000** | 9 |
| 4h | 28 | 482 | +89.5R | +0.186 | 20 | +0.355 | +0.046 | 12 |

Reading: on 15m the scanner is **zero** — 854 trades, +6R, second half negative. On 1h the first
half's edge disappears entirely in the second. Only 4h keeps a positive second half, and at +0.046R
with 12 of 28 markets agreeing it is the kind of result the cohort gate exists to test forward, not the
kind that justifies a fleet slot on a backtest. Its one fleet pair, **BTCUSD 15m**, reads −0.33R in
the first half and +0.13R in the second.

So the sentence in decision 33 — that this scanner was 81% of the fleet's edge — was true of the
leak, not the scanner. The fleet's honest backtested edge is what the other survivors carry, which
§1.4 and decision 43 show is marginal and sign-flipping. That is the finding this audit rests on:
**the fleet as chosen has no demonstrated edge, and the only path to one is forward evidence through
the shadow cohorts (§4, item 2).**
